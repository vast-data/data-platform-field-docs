def run_query(query):
    with dbapi.connect(
        driver=VAST_ADBC_DRIVER_PATH, db_kwargs= {
            "vast.db.endpoint": VASTDB_ENDPOINT,
            "vast.db.access_key": AWS_ACCESS_KEY_ID,
            "vast.db.secret_key": AWS_SECRET_ACCESS_KEY}
                                           ) as connection:
        with connection.cursor() as cursor:
            cursor.execute(query)

Similarity Search#

Metric

Best Suited Use Cases

Cosine Similarity

- Text Similarity: Document search, plagiarism detection, topic modeling (where magnitude of word counts doesn’t matter as much as the overall direction/topic).
- Recommendation Systems: Finding items or users with similar preferences, regardless of how many items they liked.
- High-Dimensional Data: Often performs well in sparse, high-dimensional spaces where magnitude variations might be less meaningful than directional alignment.

Euclidean Distance

- Image Recognition: Pixel-level comparisons or feature vector comparisons where exact spatial distance between features is important.
- Geospatial Data: Finding nearest neighbors in geographical spaces.
- Low-Dimensional Data: Generally more intuitive and effective when the number of dimensions is smaller and the absolute difference in feature values is significant.
- Anomaly Detection: Identifying data points that are “far” from clusters of normal data.

Negative Inner Product

- Neural Network Embeddings: Often used directly in models like BERT, where the output vectors are designed such that a higher inner product (and thus a less negative inner product) indicates higher similarity, combining both magnitude and direction.
- Learning-to-Rank Systems: When a model is trained to maximize this specific score for relevant pairs.
- General Similarity: In scenarios where both the direction and the magnitude of the vectors contribute meaningfully to similarity (e.g., if longer vectors represent more “intensity” or “confidence” in a certain direction).
